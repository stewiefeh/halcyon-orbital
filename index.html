The Vessel

A station built like a residence, not a ship.

Halcyon is not a spacecraft in the ordinary sense. It is a residence that happens to travel: walnut cabinetry, Irish linen, and a gallery window that runs the length of the hull.

The station completes an orbit every ninety-two minutes, which means sixteen sunrises a day. The engineering is silent. The view is not.

The Voyage

Four days. Sixteen sunrises.

I

Departure and Earthrise

You leave the coast at dusk. Ninety minutes later the sun comes up from behind the planet, and dinner is served facing it.

II

Zero-G Leisure

Mornings without weight. The long gallery becomes a pool of air, and guests learn to move again, slowly.

III

The Far Side

The station crosses to the far side of the planet, where Earth is a black disc against more stars than the eye can count.

IV

Reentry and Return

Descent over the Pacific at dawn. Champagne on the runway, and a drive home you will spend mostly silent.
